Premise

The three-second question

“Users are locals deciding right now whether to drive onto the beach.”

DESIGN-BRIEF.md

Every surface answers that as inventory: here are five ramps, here are their statuses, here is a chart.

None answers it as a verdict: go to Flagler, the tide is dropping, you have five hours of light.

Every number a verdict needs is already on the screen. Nothing on the screen ranks them.

01

tvOS

The primary surface. On in the house nearly all day, watched from across the room.

tvOS · current state

The board today

01City chip and clock. The only chrome — a good instinct, kept.
02Ramp grid, three columns, two-thirds width, glass tiles.
03Tide and weather share one card at a third of the width.
04Day timeline — the sun's rhythm across the full width.
05Cam banner at 1280×270, then the coastline rail.

Composition is sound: one hero, one grid, one strip. The problems are inside the parts, not the arrangement.

Seven palettes interpolated on the sun's real altitude, on separate rising and falling tracks, cross-fading over two seconds. Night and noon are shared so the flip at the solar extremes is seamless.

iPhone and iPad already run the same model in HeaderSky. The cross-platform language isn't missing — it exists, it's tested, and only the TV lets it fill the screen.

tvOS · the ramp grid

Five tiles of equal weight

The tile never changes. One 16px glass card, a 30px name, a 28px status line, identical padding. Status is carried entirely by a 30px icon and the color of one word.

At ten feet that reads as five equal things. A closed ramp gets exactly the visual weight of an open one, so the tile that matters is the one that looks like all the others.

The greens don't even match: #33E566 here, #10B981 on iPhone. Same semantic, two values, two files.

tvOS · when things break

Failure states, at actual size

Stale data
5h 41m of daylight left
viewModel.errorMessage renders here

An 18px line at 50% white, under the timeline caption. Every tile above it keeps showing the last-known status at full confidence — nothing signals that the board stopped listening.

Cam offline

A 22-point SF Symbol at 30% white on a 5% white fill, in a banner 270 points tall. No text, no station name, no “retrying.” The recovery logic behind it is thorough and completely invisible.

Both were built as engineering states, not designed ones. On a screen read from a couch, silence is indistinguishable from a working board.

02

The other surfaces

Web, iPhone and iPad — three personalities and two palettes that never reach the screen.

Cross-surface

Three personalities

Web

Warm cream ground, Tailwind teal chrome. The only surface with a beach personality on screen.

iPhone & iPad

Stock system grouped gray under a sun-tracking teal banner. sand50–300 is declared in AppTheme.swift and used nowhere.

tvOS

A sky gradient owns the screen. tvOcean500–800 and tvSand50 are declared in TVTheme.swift and used nowhere.

Two of the three surfaces carry a beach palette in code that never reaches a pixel. The shared language isn't absent — it's written down twice and applied once.

iPadOS · current state

A widened iPhone

01The teal banner stops at the left column's right edge. It reads as a phone header with a second column set beside it, not as a header for the page.
02The filter row clips mid-word — “Li…” — because the chips inherit a horizontally scrolling strip sized for a phone.
03Storm icons render magenta and the tide chart's Low labels indigo — SF Symbol multicolor defaults, not palette decisions.
04The left column ends halfway down and the cam rail sits below both columns, so the tallest thing on the page is empty space.
Web · current state

Same data, different grammar

01Two grammars inside one row: Open, Limited and Closed carry a border-l-4 bar and a colored numeral; Total gets a plain gray border and black type. iPhone drops Total altogether.
02The ramp name is font-bold text-sm uppercase truncate — 14px, and clipped. “International Speedway Blvd” cannot fit the card the brief's first constraint requires it to fit.
03Status is said three times per card — a 4px left bar, a tinted pill, and the status word — and none of the three is the largest thing on it.
04Every section heading is the same 14px gray uppercase label, so Recent Activity — the only narrative in the product, and web-only — weighs the same as “Live Beach Cam.”
03

Where a redesign starts

What has to survive, and the order the surfaces get done in.

Constraints

What has to survive

Glanceability Three seconds, outdoors in sun or across a room. Status and ramp name dominate at every size.
Status semantics Open green, limited amber, closed red — retuned to sit with the accent, never replaced.
Live-data tolerance “27th Av” to “International Speedway Blvd.” “Open” to “Closed for High Tide.” Five ramps to roughly thirty.
Cam aspect 1280×270 — 4.7:1, not 16:9. Downtime is normal, so the offline state is a design problem, not an edge case.
tvOS focus Tiles and coast pins need visible focus. The cam stays the hero and keeps its channel-flip feel.
Platform SwiftUI and SF Symbols on Apple, shared through BeachStatus. Tailwind and no build step on web.

Out of scope: TRMNL X and the retired Tidbyt template.

Sequence

The order of work

01 tvOS The board, the tile, the failure states. It names the palette, type scale and status tokens.
02 iPadOS Rebuild the two-column layout against the TV's grammar. Landscape included this time.
03 iPhone Carry it down to one column, and settle whether the cam comes with it.
04 Web The Modernist pass, with the activity feed promoted from a footnote to a section.
