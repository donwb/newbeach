Cam mode and Board mode, one press apart

Not a setting — a direction. Cam mode gives the video 680px, the tallest it can go before the source starts to soften. Press Down and the board rises from the bottom; the cam settles back to its 405px band and the ramps, surf and weekend fill in below. Press Up from the top row and it drops away again.

On resolution: the feed is 3222 × 680. At 1920 × 680 every source pixel maps one-to-one — the biggest sharp frame available, keeping 60% of the panorama. Filling 16:9 would have meant a 1.59× upscale of an already soft stream, which is what made the earlier full-bleed treatment mushy. Cam mode stops at native and spends the remaining 400px on the surf sentence and the five ramp cards — the tide and daylight bar stays behind in Board mode.

The rule that makes the switch legible: the verdict never moves. Brand row, weather, time and the big call sit at the same coordinates in both modes. Only the video's bottom edge travels — and the cam caption strip rides it down, exactly as it does in v3. Nothing is re-read, nothing is hunted for; information is simply added underneath.

04 The rules
Remote Up/Down are not taken over. The board has four focus rows — cam caption, ramp city heading, ramp cards, surf and weekend — and Up/Down move between them normally. Only Up with nowhere further to go, from the caption strip, falls through and closes the board; in Cam mode nothing else is focusable, so Down always opens it. Left/Right belong to the focused row, which is how cam switching, city switching and card-to-card coexist. Menu exits from Cam mode and returns to it from anywhere else.
Memory The app opens in the mode it was last left in, so the choice is made once and not re-made every launch. After 10 minutes with no remote activity it falls back to Cam mode — the picture is the better thing to leave on a television.
Interruption A ramp closing while Cam mode is up changes the verdict line in place and flashes the accent bar once. It does not open the board — a status change is not a reason to take the beach off the screen.
What holds still Brand row, weather cluster, clock, accent bar, verdict headline and its detail line. Same size, same coordinates, both modes. They are the reason the switch does not feel like a different screen.
What moves Only the video's bottom edge, 680 to 405, with the cam caption strip fixed to it. The finished board is translated up as one block behind that edge — never reflowed — so mid-rise it simply hangs below the frame. The surf sentence dissolves as the board's heading and panels arrive; the ramp cards are common to both modes and ride up with it. 340ms, ease-out, one motion.
The crop, honestly Cam mode shows 1920 of the source's 3222 columns — 60% of the panorama, centred on the pier, at 1:1. Board mode fits the full width and downscales to 0.60×. So the trade is 60% of the view at full size against 100% of it at 405px. Neither upscales.
